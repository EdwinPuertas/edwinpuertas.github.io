## Overview

Mapping Motion explores a cognitive approach to analyzing the movements of individuals with dyspraxia, a developmental coordination disorder. Movement data is captured and processed using a motion mapping system through advanced signal processing techniques, enabling the identification and quantification of characteristic movement patterns.

## Background

Dyspraxia affects approximately 5–6 % of the population and is often under-diagnosed due to the lack of objective, scalable assessment tools. Current diagnosis relies heavily on clinical observation, which is time-consuming and difficult to standardize across clinicians and institutions.

## Methodology

- **Motion Capture**: Wearable sensors and camera-based systems record full-body movement sequences
- **Signal Processing**: Raw motion signals are filtered, segmented, and normalized using custom pipelines
- **Pattern Recognition**: Deep learning models trained to distinguish dyspraxic movement profiles from typical coordination patterns
- **Cognitive Mapping**: Movement features are mapped to cognitive load indicators derived from established neuropsychological models

## Key Findings

- Identified statistically significant differences in joint coordination variability between dyspraxic and control groups
- Specific sub-movements (initiation, transition, termination) show the highest discriminative power
- The cognitive mapping layer provides interpretable markers that clinicians can incorporate into diagnosis

## Applications

- Objective diagnostic support for pediatric neurologists and occupational therapists
- Personalized rehabilitation programs based on individual movement profiles
- Assistive technologies (adaptive interfaces, motor training games) calibrated to the user's coordination profile

## Impact

The motion mapping system provides researchers and healthcare professionals with new tools for analyzing and treating dyspraxia, offering an innovative perspective that bridges cognitive neuroscience and AI.
