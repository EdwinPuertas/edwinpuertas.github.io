## Overview

A team of researchers from the Universidad Tecnológica de Bolívar developed an innovative approach to accurately calculate the Return on Investment (ROI) for photovoltaic (PV) systems. This method uniquely incorporates system degradation and maintenance factors, providing a more realistic ROI estimation compared to conventional models that assume stable performance over time.

## Problem

Standard financial models for PV investment assume constant energy output over the system's lifetime. In practice, photovoltaic panels degrade at measurable rates (typically 0.5–1 % per year), and maintenance interventions have quantifiable costs and restoration effects. Ignoring these factors leads to systematically optimistic ROI projections that misguide investment decisions.

## Methodology

- **Degradation Modeling**: Empirical degradation curves fitted to real-world field data from Colombian installations
- **Maintenance Integration**: Scheduled and corrective maintenance costs incorporated as time-series inputs
- **Financial Simulation**: Monte Carlo simulation propagates uncertainty in degradation rates and energy prices to produce ROI probability distributions
- **Validation**: Model outputs compared against historical production data from operating PV plants in the Caribbean region of Colombia

## Key Results

- Revised ROI projections differ from naive models by 8–15 % over a 20-year horizon
- Maintenance scheduling optimization reduced total lifetime cost in simulated scenarios
- Methodology generalizable to other renewable energy asset classes
