## Overview

FrameLens is a methodology using natural language processing (NLP) to analyze how news is framed in Spanish media. The study addresses the lack of research in Spanish-language media dynamics by identifying cultural and political factors that shape news narratives.

## Motivation

Most media framing research focuses on English-language sources. FrameLens fills a gap by applying computational framing analysis to Spanish-language news, where linguistic diversity and regional political contexts demand dedicated tools and corpora.

## Methodology

- **Data Collection**: Automated pipelines harvest news articles from Colombian and Mexican outlets
- **Preprocessing**: Tokenization, lemmatization, and entity normalization tuned for Latin American Spanish
- **Framing Detection**: Transformer-based classifiers identify framing categories (e.g., conflict, human interest, economic, moral, responsibility)
- **Analysis**: Statistical methods quantify variability in framing across sources, topics, and time periods

## Key Findings

- Significant variability in framing categories across different Spanish-language media environments
- Cultural and political context strongly influences which frames dominate coverage of the same event
- The *Media Framing Dataset* (Colombia & Mexico) was published as an open resource for the research community

## Impact

FrameLens enhances linguistic inclusivity in framing studies and contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of global media dynamics. The tools developed have practical implications for improving media literacy and public opinion analysis.
